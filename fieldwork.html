Field Work

Alluvial fans in cold, icy, and arid deserts are my stand-ins for Mars. These are the places and methods behind my research.

Campaigns

Jul 2024

Iceland

Þórisjökull alluvial plain; Fjallabak Nature Preserve and Höfn alluvial fan

Cold and icy Martian analog. At Þórisjökull I used Wolman pebble counts, GPR transects, infiltrometers, and eBee drone imagery to measure sediment flux and basalt abrasion. At Fjallabak and Höfn I assisted a Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum team collecting samples for VNIR spectroscopy of chemical alteration on alluvial fans.

Feb 2023; Mar 2024

Mojave Desert, California

Globe and Lucy Gray alluvial fans

Arid Martian analog. Led expeditions doing pebble counts, GPS cross-sectional transects, and UAV imagery to quantify abrasion on granitic and basaltic fans, for comparison with laboratory abrasion experiments.

Jan 2023; Dec 2024

Atacama Desert, Chile

Two field campaigns

Measured mudflow depths, compiled stratigraphic sections, collected ground-penetrating radar profiles, and mapped alluvial fan morphology to infer depositional processes and post-glacial environmental change. A second trip focused on UAV imagery of salars, luminescence geochronology samples, and developing hyperaridity as an astrobiological comparison for Mars.

Oct – Dec 2023

Svalbard

UNIS course AG-826, Arctic Quaternary Environments

Field trips around Longyearbyen to study indicators of past ice sheets, including raised beaches, moraines, and varves.

Jun – Jul 2021

Wasatch-Uinta Field Camp

Park City, Utah

Six-week field course with nine projects: stratigraphic sections, rock descriptions, regional geologic maps, and bedrock cross sections.
