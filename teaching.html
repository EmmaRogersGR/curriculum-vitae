Courses

Dartmouth College

Spring 2026

EARS 76/176: Advanced Hydrology

Teaching Assistant and Invited Lecturer

Focuses on the occurrence and movement of water at the watershed scale. Students combine GIS, modeling, field surveys, and observations to study the processes controlling runoff and streamflow generation, and the transport and fate of sediment, nutrients, and contaminants. Primary literature emphasizes methods of observation, measurement, data analysis, and prediction.

Summer 2025

JSEP: Joint Science Education Project

Mentor

An international collaboration between Greenland, the United States, and Denmark to educate and inspire the next generation of polar and STEM professionals. Since its start during the International Polar Year in 2007, high school and university students and teachers from the three countries have studied polar environments and the human dimensions of rapid Arctic change. Students practice scientific and engineering methods with mentoring from international teachers and scientists. My Greenland field work collected data for this program.

Fall 2025

EARS 45–47: Field Methods (“The Stretch”)

Teaching Assistant and Field Instructor · 25 students

An off-campus field program offered since the mid-1960s: a roughly two-month traverse across the western United States and Canada where students collect and analyze field data. I supervised field instruction for four weeks.

Spring 2024, 2025

EARS 33: Earth Surface Processes and Landforms

Teaching Assistant and Lab Instructor · 30 students

An introduction to geomorphology. Students learned river, glacial, hillslope, and wind-driven processes and the landforms they produce.

Spring 2023

EARS 34: Earth’s Biogeochemical Cycles

Teaching Assistant · 15 students

A survey of biogeochemical interactions among Earth’s crust, oceans, and atmosphere, with attention to carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, and sulfur cycles and human perturbations.
